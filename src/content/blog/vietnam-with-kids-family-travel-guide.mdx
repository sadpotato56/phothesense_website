Every week, families step into our workshops in Hanoi: parents a little jet-lagged, kids wide-eyed at the motorbikes outside. And almost every time, by the end of the session, the kids are the ones who don't want to leave.

That's the thing about Vietnam. It isn't a theme-park destination, yet children light up here. Strangers smile at them, grandmothers pinch their cheeks, street vendors slip them a free piece of fruit. The food is mild and fresh, the beaches are warm, and there is something new to touch, taste or climb around every corner.

Below are **11 of the best things to do in Vietnam with kids**, grouped by age, plus the practical tips we give every family that asks us for advice.

## Is Vietnam Good for Kids?

In one word: **yes**. But "good" isn't always "easy". The cities are fascinating for children, yet you'll juggle heavy traffic, heat and humidity, and the fatigue that comes with long days on foot.

A few rules of thumb:

* **Babies and toddlers:** keep it simple. Beaches, resorts and a few slow days in a big city with reliable air-con.
* **Younger kids (4–10):** add boat trips, historic towns, markets and hands-on activities.
* **Tweens and teens:** go for adventure: kayaking, trekking, water sports and real craft experiences.
* **Everyone:** plan a few hours of downtime every day. The midday heat wears out even the most patient child (and parent).

## Best Things to Do with Babies and Toddlers

### 1. Slow beach days on Phu Quoc

For the littlest travelers, a beach resort is hard to beat. **Phu Quoc** island has calm, warm water, resorts for every budget and direct international flights, so you can skip long transfers entirely. The central coast between Da Nang and Nha Trang also has great family beaches, but check the currents and choose a beach with lifeguards.

### 2. Easy days in Hanoi

Hanoi looks chaotic, but it is surprisingly manageable with a baby or toddler. Hotels and cafes have strong air-con, and nappies, formula and baby food are easy to buy. Build your days around short outings:

* A **water puppet show**, the perfect length for small attention spans.
* **Hoan Kiem Lake** on weekend evenings, when the streets around it close to traffic and fill with families, games and street performers.
* A cyclo (pedal rickshaw) ride through the Old Quarter.

Use a baby carrier rather than a stroller. The pavements are narrow, busy and often used for parking.

## Best Things to Do with Younger Kids

### 3. Eat your way through Hanoi's street food

Kids who are nervous about "foreign food" usually fall for Vietnamese food fast. It's rarely spicy (chili comes on the side), and there's always something familiar: crunchy spring rolls, grilled pork, noodle soup and, above all, **banh mi**.

Children love the treasure-hunt feel of a street food walk. On our **[Hanoi Banh Mi Tour](/experience/banhmi-tour)**, a local guide leads small groups (maximum 8 people) through the Old Quarter and Dong Xuan Market to the city's best sandwiches, including a legendary "ghost cart" that sells out in about three hours a day. It's lunch, a history lesson and an adventure in one, and picky eaters can choose exactly what goes into their baguette.

### 4. Turn a market into a safari

Vietnamese markets are sensory overload in the best way: pyramids of dragon fruit, baskets of live crabs, bright plastic toys and shiny charms at pocket-money prices. Give kids a mission ("find the spikiest fruit", "count the kinds of rice") and let them try rambutans, lychees and mangosteens straight from the stall.

### 5. Make your own Vietnamese coffee (yes, with kids)

Hanoi runs on coffee, and watching it being made is pure theatre: the slow drip of the *phin* filter, the thick condensed milk, the golden foam of **egg coffee** whisked by hand.

Our **[Vietnamese coffee workshop](/experience/coffee-workshop)** takes place in a 100-year-old family home in Hanoi, in intentionally small groups. Parents learn to make six iconic coffees (egg, coconut, salt and more), while kids usually take charge of the whisking and topping. Let us know your children's ages when you book, and we'll suggest how they can join in.

### 6. Step back in time in Hue

Vietnam's old imperial capital feels like a storybook. Kids can run through the gates of the **Citadel**, spot dragons and colorful guardian statues in the temples, and take a boat along the Perfume River to the royal tombs and Thien Mu Pagoda.

### 7. Lanterns and boat rides in Hoi An

Hoi An's lantern-lit old town is magical for children, especially at dusk when little boats float candles down the river. Explore by cyclo, visit the colorful assembly halls, then cool off at **An Bang beach**, only a few kilometres away.

## Best Things to Do with Tweens and Teens

### 8. Kayak among the karsts of Lan Ha Bay

Ha Long Bay's quieter neighbours, **Lan Ha Bay** and **Bai Tu Long Bay**, are perfect for active teens. Base yourselves on Cat Ba Island and paddle between limestone towers, hidden lagoons and floating fishing villages. For the bold, local operators also run rock-climbing trips on the karsts.

### 9. Water sports on the south-central coast

Teens who love the sea will be happy in **Nha Trang** (snorkelling, diving, mud baths) or **Mui Ne**, Vietnam's kitesurfing capital, where several schools teach whole families. Inland, the cool hill town of **Dalat** adds canyoning, mountain biking and waterfalls, including a bobsled ride at Datanla Falls.

### 10. Trek the rice terraces of Sapa

Sapa's green valleys, Hmong and Dao villages and misty mountain passes are a highlight of northern Vietnam. Serious hikers can tackle the trek up Fansipan (3,143 m), the country's highest peak, or ride the cable car.

Traveling with younger kids or grandparents too? Our **[easy walking tour in Sapa](/experience/sapa-easy-walking-for-kids-seniors)** is designed for all ages, with waterfalls, the Heaven Gate pass and village visits at a gentle pace.

### 11. Forge a real knife in a 400-year-old blacksmith village

For teens, few things beat the sparks, heat and noise of a real forge. In **Da Sy**, a blacksmith village on the edge of Hanoi, our **[knife-making workshop](/experience/knife-workshop)** puts a hammer in their hands. Guided by the village's only female master artisan, with over 44 years at the forge, they learn safety basics, choose their steel, then heat, hammer and shape a knife to take home.

It's a screen-free, real-world challenge teens will actually brag about, and a rare chance to see a craft village few tourists ever visit. Tell us your children's ages when booking, and we'll let you know how they can take part.

## Practical Tips for Traveling Vietnam with Kids

### Food and eating out

Vietnamese food is fresh and mostly mild, and families eat out together everywhere, so noisy kids are completely normal. Pizza and pasta are easy to find for fussy days, and tropical fruit smoothies are a daily treat. Carry hand sanitiser and stick to busy stalls with high turnover.

### What to pack

* **Baby supplies:** easy to buy in the big cities, much harder in the countryside. Stock up before leaving town.
* **Sunscreen and insect repellent:** high-factor sunscreen is expensive and not always available, so bring it from home. Use a DEET-based repellent, especially in the rainy season.
* **Carrier over stroller:** a backpack carrier with a sunshade is far more practical than a buggy. A small umbrella and a hand fan help too.
* **Warm layers in winter:** from December to February, Hanoi and Sapa can be chilly.

### Getting around

* **Car seats** are rare, so bring your own if you can and use it even for short taxi rides.
* **Trains beat buses** for families. The north–south railway stops at many favourite destinations, and kids love the sleeper cabins. Read more in [the best ways to travel around Vietnam](/blog/the-best-ways-to-travel-around-vietnam).
* **In the mountains,** hire a car with a driver, keep drives short and bring motion-sickness remedies for winding roads.
* **On boats,** insist on life jackets for everyone.

### Weather and timing

Heat, humidity and monsoon rains vary a lot by region and month. Check our guide to [the best time to visit Vietnam](/blog/best-time-to-visit-vietnam-2026) before booking, and see [18 things to know before visiting Vietnam](/blog/things-to-know-before-visiting-vietnam) for visas, cash and street-crossing tips.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Vietnam safe for families with kids?

Yes. Vietnam is generally very safe and welcoming to children. The main risks are traffic, heat and strong sea currents, so hold hands when crossing streets, plan rest breaks and swim at beaches with lifeguards.

### What is the best age to take kids to Vietnam?

Any age works if you match the trip to it. Babies and toddlers do best with beach resorts and easy city stays. From about age four, kids enjoy markets, boat trips and hands-on activities. Teens can take on trekking, kayaking and craft workshops.

### Where should families go in Vietnam?

For a first family trip, combine Hanoi with Ha Long or Lan Ha Bay and either Sapa or Ninh Binh in the north, or Hoi An and a beach in the centre. Phu Quoc is great for a pure beach holiday.

### Is Vietnamese food good for kids?

Usually, yes. Most dishes are mild, with chili served on the side. Banh mi, spring rolls, pho and grilled meats with rice are all kid favourites.

### What can kids do in Hanoi?

Watch a water puppet show, walk the car-free streets around Hoan Kiem Lake at weekends, join a street food tour, make Vietnamese coffee in a local home, or, for teens, forge a knife in a traditional blacksmith village.
