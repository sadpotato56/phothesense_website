**Short answer:** whisk 2 egg yolks with 2 tablespoons of sweetened condensed milk for about 5 minutes until thick and pale, then spoon the foam over 60 ml of very strong hot coffee. No phin? Use a moka pot, espresso or a strong French press. No condensed milk? Use sugar plus a splash of cream. It takes 10 minutes.

If you sat in a tiny Hanoi café with a warm cup in a bowl of hot water and you can't stop thinking about it, this guide is for you.

## What Is Hanoi Egg Coffee?
Egg coffee (*cà phê trứng*) is strong Vietnamese coffee topped with a thick, sweet foam of whipped egg yolk. It tastes closer to a liquid tiramisu or a warm custard than to "coffee with egg". The story most Hanoians tell: in the 1940s, when fresh milk was scarce, a bartender named Nguyễn Văn Giảng whipped egg yolks into coffee instead. His family still serves it in Hanoi today.

## Ingredients (1 Cup)
* **2 fresh egg yolks.** Use pasteurized eggs if you can (see the safety note below).
* **2 tablespoons sweetened condensed milk**, or a substitute from the table below.
* **1 teaspoon sugar** (optional, for a sweeter foam).
* **60 ml (1/4 cup) very strong hot coffee.** A dark roast works best; Vietnamese robusta is the most authentic.

**Tools:** a deep bowl, an electric hand mixer or a cheap milk frother (a whisk works too, it just takes longer), and a small cup.

## Step-by-Step Method
1. **Brew the coffee first.** Make 60 ml of very strong coffee and keep it hot.
2. **Separate the eggs.** Put only the yolks in a deep bowl. Even a little white makes the foam runny.
3. **Add sweetness.** Add the condensed milk (or substitute) and sugar.
4. **Whisk until it ribbons.** With an electric mixer or frother this takes 3–5 minutes; by hand, 8–10 minutes. Stop when the foam is pale yellow, has doubled in volume, and falls off the whisk in thick ribbons.
5. **Warm the cup.** Rinse your cup with hot water, then pour in the coffee.
6. **Top with foam.** Spoon the foam gently over the coffee so it floats.
7. **Keep it warm, Hanoi style.** Stand the cup in a small bowl of hot water. Eat the first spoonfuls of foam on their own, then stir and sip.

## No Phin? Use What You Have
The phin is just a slow drip filter. What matters is **strong, concentrated coffee**.

| You have | How to brew 60 ml for egg coffee |
|---|---|
| **Moka pot** | Closest to phin strength. Brew normally and use the first 60 ml. |
| **Espresso machine** | Pull a double shot (about 60 ml). |
| **AeroPress** | Fine grind, 18 g coffee, 80 ml water, 1 minute, press. |
| **French press** | Use double your usual coffee, steep 4–5 minutes. |
| **Instant coffee** | 2 heaped teaspoons in 60 ml just-boiled water. It works in a pinch. |
| **Pour-over / drip** | Double the coffee-to-water ratio and brew slowly. |

## No Condensed Milk? These Swaps Work
| Swap (for 2 egg yolks) | Result |
|---|---|
| **2 tbsp sugar + 1 tbsp heavy cream** | Lighter, silky foam. The easiest swap. |
| **Homemade condensed milk:** simmer 250 ml milk with 80 g sugar until reduced by half (about 30 min), cool | Closest to the real taste. Keeps a week in the fridge. |
| **1 tbsp mascarpone + 1½ tbsp sugar** | Rich and very tiramisu-like. |
| **1½ tbsp honey or maple syrup** | Thinner foam, floral flavor. Whisk a little longer. |
| **Sweetened condensed coconut milk** | Dairy-free, with a gentle coconut note. |

## Tips From Our Hanoi Coffee Table
* **Cold bowl, warm coffee.** A clean, dry, cool bowl helps the foam rise; the coffee should be as hot as possible.
* **Taste before you top.** Adjust the sugar in the foam, not in the coffee.
* **Iced version:** pour the coffee over ice and spoon the foam on top. Don't stir until you've tasted the foam.
* **Foam too runny?** Keep whisking, or add a teaspoon more condensed milk or sugar.

## A Note on Raw Egg Safety
The yolks are not cooked, so use the freshest eggs you can find, ideally **pasteurized eggs**. Pregnant women, young children, older adults and anyone with a weakened immune system should skip raw-egg drinks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does egg coffee taste like eggs?
No. Well-whisked yolks with sugar taste like custard or tiramisu cream. An "eggy" taste usually means the foam needs more whisking or more sweetness.

### Can I make egg coffee without a mixer?
Yes. Use a balloon whisk and a deep bowl, and whisk hard for 8–10 minutes. It's a good arm workout.

### What coffee is best for egg coffee?
A dark roast, ideally Vietnamese robusta, because its bold, bitter taste balances the sweet foam. A dark Italian espresso roast is a good substitute.

### Can I make it ahead?
Brew the coffee fresh. The foam is best right away; it starts to loosen after about 15 minutes.
