Vietnam is one of the best-value destinations in the world. A steaming bowl of pho costs less than a coffee back home, a comfortable hotel room costs less than a dinner out in London or Sydney, and a cross-town ride rarely costs more than a couple of dollars.

But "cheap" doesn't mean you should stop thinking about money. Knowing a few basics (what things *should* cost, when you need cash, and how to avoid the common tourist mark-ups) makes your trip smoother and lets you spend on the things that matter.

Here's our local guide to **travel costs and money in Vietnam in 2026**, from Hanoi.

> Prices below are typical ranges in 2026 and vary by city, season and neighbourhood. As a rough guide, **US$1 ≈ 25,000–26,000 VND**. Check a live exchange rate before you travel.

## The Vietnamese Dong (VND) in 60 Seconds

* Vietnam's currency is the **dong (VND, written "đ" or "₫")**.
* Notes range from 1,000đ to 500,000đ, and coins are no longer used.
* The **20,000đ (blue) and 500,000đ (blue-grey) notes look alike**. Check before you hand one over.
* Prices are often shortened: "50k" means 50,000đ.
* A quick mental trick: **100,000đ is roughly US$4**, so 500,000đ is about US$20.

## Cash, Cards and ATMs

### Cash is still king (outside hotels and malls)

Hotels, supermarkets, chain cafes and many restaurants accept Visa and Mastercard, especially in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. But street food, markets, small shops, homestays and many taxis still take **cash only**. Always carry some small notes (10,000đ to 100,000đ). Vendors often can't break a 500,000đ note for a 30,000đ snack.

### Using ATMs

* ATMs are everywhere, even in small towns.
* Most charge a **fee of roughly 30,000–60,000đ** per withdrawal, on top of whatever your home bank charges.
* Many cap each withdrawal (often 2–5 million đ). Need more? Just withdraw again.
* Use ATMs attached to **banks** (Vietcombank, BIDV, TPBank, VPBank and others) and do it during the day, so it's easy to get help if the machine keeps your card.
* When the machine offers to convert to your home currency, **decline** and choose to be charged in VND. It's almost always cheaper.

### Exchanging money

US dollars and euros are easy to exchange at banks and at the gold shops around Hanoi's Old Quarter, which often give better rates than the airport. Bring clean, undamaged notes, since torn bills may be refused.

### Mobile payments

Vietnamese people increasingly pay by scanning QR codes with local banking apps. As a visitor you usually can't use these without a Vietnamese bank account, so rely on cash and cards. **Grab** lets you pay by card in the app, which is handy for rides and food delivery.

## Tipping in Vietnam

Tipping isn't part of everyday local life, but it's appreciated:

* **Street food and local restaurants:** not expected. Rounding up is a nice gesture.
* **Upscale restaurants and bars:** check whether a service charge is already included. If not, 5–10% is generous.
* **Tour guides and drivers:** a tip is customary if you're happy with the service, for example 100,000–200,000đ per person for a day tour.
* **Spa and massage staff:** often expect a tip, sometimes stated on the price list.

## Haggling and Avoiding Tourist Prices

* **Markets and street souvenirs:** friendly haggling is normal. Start around half the first price, smile, and be ready to walk away.
* **Food stalls, cafes, supermarkets and shops with price tags:** prices are fixed, so no haggling.
* **Ask before you eat.** At stalls without a menu, ask "*bao nhiêu tiền?*" (how much?) before ordering.
* **Use Grab or Xanh SM** instead of hailing random taxis or motorbikes, so you see the price upfront.
* **Count your change** and watch for the look-alike 20,000đ / 500,000đ notes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Vietnam expensive for tourists?

No. Vietnam is one of the most affordable destinations in Asia. Budget travelers can get by on US$25–40 a day, and US$60–120 a day buys a comfortable mid-range trip.

### Should I bring cash or use cards in Vietnam?

Both. Cards work in hotels, malls and many restaurants in big cities, but you'll need cash for street food, markets, small shops and rural areas. ATMs are easy to find.

### How much cash should I carry per day?

For most travelers, 500,000–1,000,000đ (about US$20–40) a day in small notes covers food, drinks and small purchases. Pay for hotels and bigger bookings by card.

### Do you tip in Vietnam?

It isn't expected in local restaurants or taxis, but it's appreciated. Tour guides, drivers and spa staff usually expect a small tip.

### What's the best thing to spend money on in Vietnam?

Food and experiences. Street food is cheap and delicious, and hands-on experiences, like making Vietnamese coffee or forging a knife in a blacksmith village, give you memories no souvenir can.
